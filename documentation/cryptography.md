# Cryptographic Specification & Mathematical Proofs

## The Schnorr Zero-Knowledge Identification Protocol

### 1. Mathematical Framework

The protocol operates in a cyclic subgroup $\langle g \rangle$ of $\mathbb{Z}_p^*$ with prime order $q$.

#### Public Parameters:
- $p = 23$: A prime modulus such that $q \mid (p - 1)$. Here, $23 - 1 = 22 = 2 \times 11$.
- $q = 11$: The prime order of the subgroup generated by $g$.
- $g = 2$: A generator of order $q$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p^*$.

Let us verify the subgroup generated by $g = 2$ modulo $p = 23$:
$$\begin{aligned}
2^1 \pmod{23} &= 2 \\
2^2 \pmod{23} &= 4 \\
2^3 \pmod{23} &= 8 \\
2^4 \pmod{23} &= 16 \\
2^5 \pmod{23} &= 32 \equiv 9 \\
2^6 \pmod{23} &= 18 \\
2^7 \pmod{23} &= 36 \equiv 13 \\
2^8 \pmod{23} &= 26 \equiv 3 \\
2^9 \pmod{23} &= 6 \\
2^{10} \pmod{23} &= 12 \\
2^{11} \pmod{23} &= 24 \equiv 1
\end{aligned}$$

Thus, $\text{ord}(g) = 11 = q$. The cyclic group is:
$$\langle 2 \rangle = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12, 13, 16, 18\} \subset \mathbb{Z}_{23}^*$$

### 2. Protocol Specification

#### Step 1: Secret Setup & Key Generation
- Prover selects or derives secret scalar witness $x \in \mathbb{Z}_q^* = \{1, 2, \dots, q-1\}$.
- Prover computes public key:
  $$y = g^x \pmod p$$
- Prover publishes $y$. $x$ remains private.

#### Step 2: Commitment
- Prover chooses fresh random nonce $r \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{Z}_q^*$.
- Prover computes commitment:
  $$t = g^r \pmod p$$
- Prover sends $t$ to Verifier.

#### Step 3: Challenge
- Verifier chooses random challenge $e \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{Z}_q$.
- Verifier sends $e$ to Prover.

#### Step 4: Response
- Prover computes scalar response:
  $$z = (r + e \cdot x) \pmod q$$
- Prover sends $z$ to Verifier.

#### Step 5: Verification
- Verifier checks:
  $$g^z \pmod p \stackrel{?}{=} (t \cdot y^e) \pmod p$$
- If equal, Verifier accepts (**PROOF ACCEPTED**); otherwise, rejects (**PROOF REJECTED**).

### 3. Formal Cryptographic Proofs

#### 3.1 Proof of Completeness
**Theorem:** An honest prover who possesses witness $x$ corresponding to public key $y = g^x \pmod p$ will always satisfy the verification equation.

**Proof:**
By definition, the response is $z \equiv r + e \cdot x \pmod q$.
Therefore, $z = r + e \cdot x + k \cdot q$ for some integer $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Evaluating the left-hand side:
$$\text{LHS} = g^z \pmod p = g^{r + e \cdot x + k \cdot q} \pmod p$$

By the properties of modular exponentiation in cyclic group $\langle g \rangle$ where $\text{ord}(g) = q$:
$$g^{k \cdot q} \equiv (g^q)^k \equiv 1^k \equiv 1 \pmod p$$

Therefore:
$$\text{LHS} = g^r \cdot (g^x)^e \cdot 1 \pmod p$$

Substituting $t = g^r \pmod p$ and $y = g^x \pmod p$:
$$\text{LHS} = t \cdot y^e \pmod p = \text{RHS}$$

$\text{LHS} \equiv \text{RHS} \pmod p$, completing the proof. $\blacksquare$

---

#### 3.2 Proof of Soundness (Special Soundness & Knowledge Extractor)
**Theorem:** A cheating prover without knowledge of $x$ cannot convince the verifier, except with probability at most $1/q$.

**Proof via Knowledge Extractor:**
Suppose an adversary $\mathcal{A}$ can produce valid responses for two different challenges $e_1 \ne e_2$ using the *same* commitment $t$.

Then:
$$\begin{aligned}
g^{z_1} &\equiv t \cdot y^{e_1} \pmod p \\
g^{z_2} &\equiv t \cdot y^{e_2} \pmod p
\end{aligned}$$

Dividing the two equations:
$$\frac{g^{z_1}}{g^{z_2}} \equiv \frac{t \cdot y^{e_1}}{t \cdot y^{e_2}} \equiv y^{e_1 - e_2} \pmod p$$
$$g^{z_1 - z_2} \equiv (g^x)^{e_1 - e_2} \equiv g^{x(e_1 - e_2)} \pmod p$$

In the exponent space modulo $q$:
$$z_1 - z_2 \equiv x(e_1 - e_2) \pmod q$$

Since $q$ is prime and $e_1 \ne e_2$, $(e_1 - e_2)$ has a modular multiplicative inverse modulo $q$:
$$x = (z_1 - z_2) \cdot (e_1 - e_2)^{-1} \pmod q$$

Thus, a knowledge extractor can extract the exact secret $x$ in polynomial time from two successful runs. Since the adversary does not know $x$, they can answer at most *one* challenge correctly for any commitment $t$. Therefore, their probability of guessing is bounded by $1/q$. $\blacksquare$

---

#### 3.3 Proof of Zero-Knowledge (Honest-Verifier Zero-Knowledge Simulator)
**Theorem:** The protocol leaks zero information about $x$ to an honest verifier.

**Proof via Simulator:**
We construct a polynomial-time Simulator $\mathcal{S}$ that does *not* know $x$, yet produces transcripts $(t', e', z')$ whose probability distribution is identical to real transcripts.

Algorithm $\mathcal{S}$:
1. Choose challenge $e' \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{Z}_q$ uniformly at random.
2. Choose response $z' \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{Z}_q$ uniformly at random.
3. Compute commitment backwards:
   $$t' = g^{z'} \cdot (y^{e'})^{-1} \pmod p$$

Verification check on simulated transcript:
$$t' \cdot y^{e'} = \left(g^{z'} \cdot (y^{e'})^{-1}\right) \cdot y^{e'} = g^{z'} \pmod p$$

The simulated tuple $(t', e', z')$ perfectly satisfies the verification equation. In both real and simulated executions:
- $e$ is uniform in $\mathbb{Z}_q$.
- $z$ is uniform in $\mathbb{Z}_q$.
- $t$ is uniquely determined by $g^z \cdot y^{-e} \pmod p$.

Because the simulated transcript is statistically indistinguishable from a real conversation, an observer learns nothing about $x$. $\blacksquare$

### 4. Step-by-Step Educational Calculation ($p=23, q=11, g=2$)

Suppose:
- Alice's Secret: $x = 3$
- Public Key: $y = 2^3 \pmod{23} = 8$
- Alice's Nonce: $r = 5$
- Commitment: $t = 2^5 \pmod{23} = 32 \pmod{23} = 9$
- Bob's Challenge: $e = 4$

Alice computes response:
$$z = (r + e \cdot x) \pmod{11} = (5 + 4 \times 3) \pmod{11} = 17 \pmod{11} = 6$$

Bob verifies:
$$\text{LHS} = 2^6 \pmod{23} = 64 \pmod{23} = 18$$
$$\text{RHS} = (t \cdot y^e) \pmod{23} = (9 \cdot 8^4) \pmod{23}$$
$$8^2 = 64 \equiv 18 \pmod{23}$$
$$8^4 = 18^2 = 324 = 14 \times 23 + 2 \equiv 2 \pmod{23}$$
$$\text{RHS} = 9 \times 2 = 18 \pmod{23}$$

Since $\text{LHS} = 18$ and $\text{RHS} = 18$, **LHS === RHS**! Verification succeeds!
